# 02 dedup-arch

## Summary
Dedup costs **2,561 ms measured at 60M** (`00-profile.md`), and its per-bucket closure is **37.1% of all user-CPU samples**; the cost is 60M random 64-byte table writes plus two slot-order survivor traversals with **one** `PersonMap` probe and one `out` push per valid survivor. The best fit for the freeze is a compact dedup table with **exact `u128` keys** (`HashMap<u128, u32>` over a dense `EvRaw` payload) plus one table reused per task: est. **300–500 ms** off, 3–4 h, low risk. A global concurrent winner table (design b) and two-pass parse (design c) are rejected on cost grounds, not effort alone.

## Current cost
Measured (60M, serial, `00-profile.md`): dedup **2,561 ms**, 402,608 minor faults (≈1.6 GB of fresh pages), 4.35× the 20M time (589 ms); `closure#5` (whole P2 bucket body, inlined) **3,171/8,541 samples = 37.1%**; `mi_page_free_list_extend` 6.9% (fresh-page init, largely P2's tables). `00-profile.md` is one observation and disagrees with `README.md` §1 (2,712 ms) by ~6%, so treat the phase as **2.5–2.8 s**.

Cost model (estimate, bounded). 8,541 samples at 250 Hz ≈ 34 CPU-s of user time; 37.1% ≈ **12.7 CPU-s ≈ 1.95 s of the phase wall** at ~6.5 busy threads, i.e. ≈210 ns CPU per copy:
- **Insert** – 60M copies into 256 `HashMap<u128, EvRaw>` pre-sized per bucket (`src/v2/mod.rs:215`). Key `u128` (align 16) + `EvRaw` 48 B (align 16) ⇒ **64 B/element + ~1 B control ≈ 65 B**; 234k copies/bucket ⇒ `with_capacity` rounds to 2^19 slots ≈ **34 MB/bucket** — 34× a Cascade Lake core's 1 MiB L2 and 6× the 4-core L3 slice. ~70–90 ns each (control line + entry line, most probes miss) ≈ 4.2–5.4 CPU-s.
- **Survivor pass** (`mod.rs:238-256`) – two slot-order walks of the same ~56M valid survivors (count, then fill). The **person probe happens once per survivor**, in the count pass, and its result is written back into the map value's `user` field (`mod.rs:242`), so the fill pass reads the person without a second lookup. Traversals: 2 × 3.4 GB slot-order ≈ 1.1–1.7 CPU-s. One probe of `PersonMap` (`people.rs:18`) at 60–100 ns: ~2.3M entries — `plan.rs:147` gives 2M users at 60M, alias mix `plan.rs:185-193` averages ≈2.16 IDs/person, minus the name itself ⇒ *(u128,u128)* = 32 B, 2^22 slots ≈ **134 MB** — so 56M probes ≈ **3.4–5.6 CPU-s ≈ 0.5–0.9 s of wall**, with no reuse locality (2M distinct IDs).
- **Output push** into 256 `out` `Vec`s: 56M × 32 B ≈ 1.8 GB of semi-random writes ≈ 0.5–0.8 CPU-s.

`rules.md:51` fixes the semantics every design must keep: dedup precedes validation, min `ingest_ts` wins, equal-ingest copies are identical, malformed copies were dropped in parse and can never win. `rules.md:121`: 2–4% of events are duplicated. `parse.rs:24-76` already returns early for invalid copies *after* computing `ingest_s`, which is why design (c) has nothing to gain.

## Details
**Design (a) — partitioned local compact table (= #1+#2+#3).** Each task owns its buckets, so no synchronisation; radix partitioning by `id::mix` top bits (`id.rs:52-57`) already gives the independence, and this is DuckDB's two-part aggregate layout: a slot array of small entries over separately stored payload. The compact table is what pays: it replaces 60M random 64 B read-modify-writes with 60M 8–33 B slot touches plus sequential appends, and makes both survivor walks sequential. Splitting dedup bits from person bits (`SHOPT_DEDUP_BITS=10–11`) is safe here because the person side stays at 256; the measured regression at `SHOPT_BUCKET_BITS=12` (SYNTHESIS §2) was the person side (`nb²` `Vec`s), not dedup.

**Design (b) — global concurrent winner table, rejected.** Two independent problems. First, `fetch_min` cannot answer "am I the winner": a later thread may still beat you, so a confirm pass over all 60M copies is mandatory — 60M read-modify-writes plus 60M probes, versus today's 60M inserts plus 56M slot-order reads. Second, exactness needs the full `u128` key compared atomically: x86 has 16 B CAS (`cmpxchg16b`) only with alignment and manual loop construction, or a seqlock/poison scheme, so the whole table would be hand-written and unsafe. What it *does* win is removing the 2.9 GB id-ordered `EvRaw` scatter (append instead). Honest range: **−1.0 s to +0.3 s, 12–16 h** — not a freeze candidate, and its "winner tag" scheme would need unchecked indexing, which this report does not propose.

**Design (c) — two-pass parse, rejected.** Pass 1 needs `event_id` + `ingest_ts` (`parse.rs:30-47`) and still splits every line; 96–98% of copies survive dedup, so pass 2 reparses ~58M lines plus 58M random mmap re-reads. It strictly worsens `links+events` (2.8–3.4 s). The only field work it could skip is already skipped for invalid copies.

**Design (d) — radix-sort dedup, deferred.** Per bucket, radix-sort `(mix(id), idx)` on 12–16 B records (3.8 MB, L3-resident), then take the min-ingest head of each equal-hash run with a full-id compare. Round 1 (`04-dedup.md` idea 4) bounded it at 0–1.5 s unmeasured; Balkesen et al. (VLDB 7:85) find radix-hash beats sort for main-memory joins. Not additive with #1/#2. Keep as the A/B only if #1+#2 land early.

**Person probes.** They are 0.5–0.9 s of the phase and are *not* touched by #1–#4. Making them cache-friendly needs a prefetch-friendly map of our own (compact `u64` hash + `u32` index, exact verify, safe indexing) — 150–350 ms, 3–4 h, and it also serves the clicks phase (`mod.rs` click scatter) — but it cannot be done against hashbrown's internals, so it is out of scope here; note it as the next dedup card after the freeze.

## Rejected ideas
- *Bloom filter / hash-only dedup*: cannot decide min ingest; ignoring the hash is inexact (`04-dedup.md` validation note).
- *Defer `user`/`price` parsing for suspected duplicates*: duplicates are only identified after the min-ingest decision.
- *One-pass fill with estimated capacities*: saves ~half a traversal (80–140 ms) but risks `Vec` doubling of 1.8 GB; not worth the memory variance.
- *Dense per-bucket person index for dedup output*: must preserve `u128` person order for the `sessions.rs` merge.
- *Click dedup*: D7 already drops it (`PLAN.md:99`).
- *Raising `SHOPT_BUCKET_BITS` alone*: measured regression (SYNTHESIS §2).

## Dependencies / conflicts
#2 needs #1's payload and index. #3 restructures the same P2 lines; land it first or merge. #4 follows #1/#3 to avoid rework. #1/#2 cut peak anon (dedup tables ~3.8 → ~1.0 GB over the phase), helping T15's ≤10 GB gate (`PLAN.md:348`). #1/#3 change `ev_out` shape, so `sessions-60m` (05) and `critical-path` (06) re-measure; `memory-traffic` (04) assumes today's table size.

## Recommended card
**S4-3′ — Exact-key compact event dedup, one table per task** · Scope: `src/v2/mod.rs` (P2 only) · Depends: S4-1 (or today's `ev_parts`) · **3–4 h** · est. **300–500 ms** at 60M.

- In P2, per bucket task: `payload: Vec<EvRaw>` (pre-sized from `total`), `kept: HashMap<u128, u32>` (`with_capacity(total)`), both reused across that task's buckets via `clear()` (bucket tasks grouped with a `fold` so one task handles several buckets).
- Insert: append the copy, `payload.len() - 1` as `u32`; on `Occupied(old)`, `let old = old.get();` and overwrite `payload[*old as usize]` in place **only** if `r.ingest_s < payload[*old as usize].ingest_s`. Equal `ingest_s` ⇒ copies identical ⇒ keep the first.
- Survivor walks iterate `payload` sequentially (twice: count, then fill), skipping `flags & VALID == 0` *after* the winner is fixed (E5), mapping person once in the count pass and writing it back into the payload (E4: the winner's own `user`/`price`/`flags` are what get output).
- Accept: `cargo test`; `make fixtures`/`check-dev` 36/36; `diff-seeds SEEDS=2-30`; seeds 41–43 at 2M; G4 `make oracle` + `MATCH` on p20; G1 also with `SHOPT_BUCKET_BITS=1` and `SHOPT_CHUNK_BYTES=100`; record `dedup` ms, minor faults and `RssAnon` at 60M.
- Traps: (1) never dedup on a hash — the map key stays the full `u128`; (2) use safe indexing only, with a `debug_assert!(idx < payload.len())`; a wrong index must panic, not corrupt; (3) do not "upgrade" to a later valid copy (E5); (4) a bad payload capacity estimate may cost a `Vec` growth, never correctness; (5) `out` order stays irrelevant (`sessions.rs:187` sorts) but every survivor appears exactly once — assert survivor count == sum of `out` lengths under `debug_assertions`; (6) measure at 60M: the 4.35× scaling is the entire reason this phase is worth a card.
